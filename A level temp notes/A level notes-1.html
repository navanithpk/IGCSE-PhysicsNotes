Here are some in-depth notes on measurements, focusing on exam-style questions, in HTML format wrapped in MathJax using \\[ \\] and \\( \\). ### Physical Quantities and Units * **Define a physical quantity.** * * **What are the SI base quantities and their units?** * The SI base quantities and their units are: * Mass (kg) * Length (m) * Time (s) * Current (A) * Temperature (K) * **Express derived units as products or quotients of the SI base units.** * Derived units are formed by combining base units. For example, velocity is a derived unit. It can be expressed as meters per second, or \\( \frac{m}{s} \\). * **What are the prefixes used to indicate decimal submultiples or multiples of units and what are their symbols?** * pico (p), nano (n), micro (μ), milli (m), centi (c), deci (d), kilo (k), mega (M), giga (G) and tera (T) * **Convert between units with different prefixes.** * For example, convert 1 km/h to m/s: * 1 km = 1000 m * 1 h = 3600 s * Therefore, 1 km/h = \\( \frac{1000 \ m}{3600 \ s} \\) = 0.28 m/s ### Errors and Uncertainties * **What are systematic errors and give examples?** * Systematic errors are errors that occur consistently in the same direction. They can be caused by faulty equipment or by the way the experiment is conducted. Examples include: * **Zero error:** This occurs when a measuring instrument does not read zero when it should. For example, a balance that reads 0.1 g when it is empty has a zero error of +0.1 g. * **Parallax error:** This occurs when a measurement is taken from an angle, rather than from directly in front of the scale. For example, if you read a thermometer from the side, you will get a different reading than if you read it from directly in front. * **What are random errors and give examples?** * Random errors are errors that occur randomly and are unpredictable. They are often caused by human error or by fluctuations in the environment. For example, if you are timing how long it takes a ball to fall from a certain height, your reaction time will introduce random error into your measurements. * **Distinguish between precision and accuracy.** * **Accuracy** refers to how close a measurement is to the true value. * **Precision** refers to how close a set of measurements are to each other. * **Assess the uncertainty in a derived quantity by simple addition of absolute or percentage uncertainties.** * **Absolute uncertainty:** The absolute uncertainty of a measurement is the smallest division on the measuring instrument. For example, if you are measuring the length of a piece of paper with a ruler that has millimeter markings, the absolute uncertainty of your measurement is ±0.5 mm. * **Percentage uncertainty:** The percentage uncertainty of a measurement is the absolute uncertainty divided by the measurement, multiplied by 100%. For example, if you measure the length of a piece of paper to be 20.0 cm with an absolute uncertainty of ±0.5 mm, the percentage uncertainty is: \\( \frac{0.5 \ mm}{200 \ mm} \times 100\% = 0.25\% \\) ### Scalars and Vectors * **Define scalar and vector quantities and give examples of each.** * A **scalar** quantity has magnitude only. For example, mass, speed, and temperature are scalar quantities. * A **vector** quantity has both magnitude and direction. For example, displacement, velocity, and force are vector quantities. * **Add and subtract coplanar vectors graphically.** * **Head-to-tail method:** To add two vectors, draw the first vector to scale. Then, draw the second vector with its tail at the head of the first vector. The resultant vector is the vector drawn from the tail of the first vector to the head of the second vector. * To subtract two vectors, add the negative of the second vector to the first vector. The negative of a vector has the same magnitude as the original vector but points in the opposite direction. * **Parallelogram method:** To add two vectors, draw the vectors to scale with their tails at the same point. Then, complete the parallelogram. The resultant vector is the diagonal of the parallelogram drawn from the common tail of the two vectors. * **Add and subtract vectors at right angles to each other mathematically using Pythagoras’ theorem.** * If two vectors are at right angles to each other, the magnitude of the resultant vector can be found using Pythagoras' theorem: * \\( |R|^2 = |A|^2 + |B|^2 \\) * The direction of the resultant vector can be found using trigonometry. * **Resolve a vector into two perpendicular components.** * To resolve a vector into two perpendicular components, draw the vector to scale. Then, draw two perpendicular lines from the tail of the vector, one horizontal and one vertical. The horizontal and vertical components of the vector are the projections of the vector onto the horizontal and vertical lines, respectively.

Unit 1: Physical Quantities

Physics is the branch of science concerned with the nature and properties of matter and energy.

Definition: Physical quantities are properties of a material or system that can be measured and expressed in numerical values with units. They are categorized into two types: Scalar quantities and Vector Quantities.

Types of Physical Quantities:

  1. Scalar Quantities: These have only magnitude (size) and no direction.
    • Examples: Mass, Speed, Time, Temperature, Energy

  2. Vector Quantities: These have both magnitude and direction.
    • Examples: Velocity, Displacement, Force, Acceleration

Fundamental vs. Derived Quantities:

  1. Fundamental Quantities: : Cannot be derived from other physical quantities.
    • Examples: Length, Mass, Time, Electric Current, Temperature, Amount of Substance, Luminous Intensity

  2. Derived Quantities: Formed by combining fundamental quantities.
    • Examples: Speed (m/s), Force (N = kg m/s²), Work (J = kg m²/s²)

Did you know? The speed of light is approximately 299,792,458 meters per second!

3. Errors and Uncertainties

Types of Errors:

  • Systematic Errors: Caused by faulty instruments, calibration errors, or incorrect methods. Example: A scale that always reads 5g more than the actual value.
  • Random Errors: Caused by unpredictable variations in the experiment. Example: Human reaction time variations in stopwatch readings.

Reducing Errors:

  • For Systematic Errors: Use properly calibrated instruments.
  • For Random Errors: Take multiple measurements and calculate the average.

Uncertainties in Measurements:

  • Absolute Uncertainty: The range within which the true value is expected to lie.
  • Percentage Uncertainty: Expresses uncertainty as a percentage of the measured value.

Did You Know?

The formula for kinetic energy is given by: \[ KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \] where \( m \) is mass and \( v \) is velocity.

4. Vectors and Scalars

Differences Between Scalars and Vectors:

PropertyScalarVector
MagnitudeYesYes
DirectionNoYes
ExamplesSpeed, Mass, TemperatureVelocity, Displacement, Force

Vector Representation:

  • Represented by an arrow.
  • Length of the arrow = magnitude.
  • Arrow direction = direction of the vector.

Vector Addition:

  • Graphical Method (Head-to-Tail Rule): Draw the first vector, connect the second vector’s tail to the first, and draw the resultant vector.
  • Analytical Method (Using Components): Break each vector into components and use Pythagoras’ theorem to find magnitude.

Vector Subtraction:

Reverse the direction of the vector to be subtracted and then add using the head-to-tail rule.

Applications of Vectors in Physics:

  • Force Analysis: Resolving forces into components.
  • Projectile Motion: Splitting motion into horizontal and vertical components.
  • Equilibrium: Analyzing net force to determine if an object is in equilibrium.

Summary

  • Physical quantities are classified as scalars and vectors.
  • SI units ensure standardization in scientific measurements.
  • Errors and uncertainties affect accuracy and precision.
  • Vectors differ from scalars due to their directional component.
